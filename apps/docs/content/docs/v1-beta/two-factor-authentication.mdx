Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second step to sign-in: after your password, you also enter a
one-time code from an authenticator app. Ouitransfer uses **time-based one-time passwords (TOTP,
RFC 6238)**, compatible with apps such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, and
1Password.

2FA is opt-in and configured per user from your own account. There is no global toggle: each user
enables it for themselves.

## Enabling 2FA

You manage 2FA from your **Profile** page (your account settings).

<Steps>
<Step>
**Start setup.** Open your Profile and begin two-factor setup. Ouitransfer generates a secret and
displays a QR code.
</Step>
<Step>
**Add the account to your authenticator.** Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or enter
the displayed key manually. The codes are 6 digits and rotate every 30 seconds.
</Step>
<Step>
**Confirm.** Enter the current 6-digit code from your app together with your **account password**,
then confirm. The password step ensures someone who only borrowed your open session cannot enroll
their own device.
</Step>
<Step>
**Save your backup codes.** Ouitransfer shows **10 single-use backup codes**. Store them somewhere
safe (a password manager works well). They are displayed **only once** and are your fallback if you
lose access to your authenticator.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warn">
Enabling 2FA signs out your other active sessions. You will need to sign back in (with your new
second factor) on your other devices.
</Callout>

## Signing in with 2FA

After you enter your username and password, Ouitransfer prompts for your second factor. Enter the
current 6-digit code from your authenticator app, or one of your **backup codes** if you do not have
your authenticator at hand.

### Trusted devices

When prompted, you can mark the current device as trusted. A trusted device skips the 2FA prompt for
**30 days**, after which you are asked again. You can review and remove trusted devices from your
Profile at any time, and all trusted devices are revoked automatically when you disable 2FA.

## Backup codes

Backup codes let you sign in when your authenticator is unavailable.

- You receive **10 codes** when you enable 2FA. Each code works **once**.
- Your Profile shows how many unused codes remain.
- You can **regenerate** a fresh set at any time. Regenerating requires your password and a current
  authenticator (or backup) code, and it invalidates the previous set.

<Callout type="warn">
Backup codes are the only way back in if you lose your authenticator. Ouitransfer has no
self-service or administrator "reset 2FA" action: if you lose **both** your authenticator and all of
your backup codes, an operator must clear the 2FA flag directly in the server database to restore
access. Keep your codes safe.
</Callout>

## Disabling 2FA

You can turn 2FA off from your Profile. For safety this is a step-up action: it requires your
**password and a current authenticator or backup code**. Disabling 2FA also revokes all of your
trusted devices and signs out your other sessions.

## How it is protected

- **TOTP parameters:** SHA-1, 6 digits, 30-second period, with a one-step tolerance on either side
  for clock drift (RFC 6238).
- **Encrypted at rest:** the TOTP secret is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using the server's
  `ENCRYPTION_SECRET`, so a database leak does not expose the seed.
- **Hashed backup codes:** backup codes are stored as keyed HMAC-SHA256 hashes, never in plaintext,
  and are matched in constant time.
- **Replay protection:** a one-time code cannot be reused. Once a time-step is consumed at login it
  is rejected if presented again.

<Callout type="info">
2FA is independent of single sign-on. When you sign in through an [OIDC / SSO provider](/docs/v1-beta/oidc-authentication),
the second factor is handled by that provider, so Ouitransfer's TOTP setup applies to
password-based accounts.
</Callout>
